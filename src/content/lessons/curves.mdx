## The most powerful tool in editing

A **<Term id="curves">tone curve</Term>** maps input brightness (bottom axis) to output brightness (side axis). The straight diagonal means "no change". Bend it and you remap tones:

- **Raise a point**: those tones get brighter.
- **Lower a point**: those tones get darker.
- **Steeper section**: more contrast in that tonal range.
- **Flatter section**: less contrast there.

The left of the graph is shadows, the middle is midtones, the right is highlights.

## Three curves every editor uses

**1. The S-curve: contrast.** Pull the shadows (lower-left quarter) down a little and push the highlights (upper-right quarter) up. The middle gets steeper: more contrast, more "pop".

**2. Faded / matte blacks.** Drag the bottom-left end point straight *up*. Pure black becomes dark grey, giving that film or Instagram look.

**3. Brightness without blowing highlights.** Lift just the middle of the curve. Midtones brighten while black and white points stay put.

## Color curves

Switch the channel to **R**, **G** or **B** and the curve affects only that color:

- **Blue curve: lift the shadows** → blue/teal shadows. **Pull the highlights down** → warmer, yellow highlights. That's a popular cinematic look.
- **Red curve: lift slightly** → warmer skin and sunsets.
- **Green curve: lower the midtones slightly** → magenta tint, a fashion look.

<Mistakes>
- Points too close together, which causes harsh, posterized transitions. Small moves go a long way.
- Making the curve go *down* as it moves right. That inverts tones (fun for effects, not for photos).
</Mistakes>
