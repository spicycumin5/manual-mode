## You don't have to do everything manually

"Manual mode" is a mindset, not a dial position: **you decide what matters, and you make sure the camera respects it.** Semi-automatic modes are tools for that.

| Mode | You set | Camera sets | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M** (Manual) | Aperture, shutter, ISO | Nothing (the meter just advises) | Consistent light: studio, night, panoramas, flash |
| **M + Auto ISO** | Aperture, shutter | ISO | Sports, events, wildlife: full creative control, changing light |
| **Av / A** (Aperture priority) | Aperture (+ ISO) | Shutter | Portraits, landscapes: when depth of field matters most |
| **Tv / S** (Shutter priority) | Shutter (+ ISO) | Aperture | Motion: action, panning, waterfalls |
| **P** (Program) | (Optional) ISO, compensation | Aperture + shutter | Snapshots, when you just need *a* photo |

In every auto mode, **exposure compensation** is how you overrule the meter.

## Which should I use?

A practical habit many pros use:

- **Av** most of the day, with Auto ISO and a minimum shutter speed set.
- **M + Auto ISO** for action (lock the shutter to freeze motion, lock the aperture for the look).
- **Full M** whenever the light is constant or every frame must match: night, studio, flash, panoramas, time-lapses.
