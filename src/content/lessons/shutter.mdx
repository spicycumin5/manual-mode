## Time as a creative tool

The **<Term id="shutter-speed">shutter speed</Term>** is how long the sensor collects light. It is written in seconds or fractions: 1/1000 is one-thousandth of a second; 2s or 2″ is two whole seconds.

Each halving or doubling is one stop: **1/1000 · 1/500 · 1/250 · 1/125 · 1/60 · 1/30 · 1/15 · 1/8…**

Anything that moves during the exposure smears. That is either a problem (blurry kids) or the whole point (silky waterfalls, light trails).

## How fast is fast enough?

| Subject | To freeze it |
|---|---|
| People walking | 1/250 s |
| Running, kids playing | 1/500 s |
| Sports, birds in flight | 1/1000 – 1/2000 s |
| Water drops, splashes | 1/2000 s + |

To *show* motion instead: waterfalls look silky from about ½ s; car light trails need several seconds.

## Camera shake

There are two kinds of blur. **<Term id="motion-blur">Motion blur</Term>** is the subject moving. **<Term id="camera-shake">Camera shake</Term>** is *you* moving, and it blurs the whole frame.

The **<Term id="reciprocal-rule">reciprocal rule</Term>**: handheld, use at least **1 ÷ focal length** seconds. A 50mm lens needs 1/50 s; a 200mm lens needs 1/200 s. On a crop-sensor camera, multiply the focal length by the crop factor first.

**<Term id="ibis">Image stabilization</Term>** buys you extra stops: with 4 stops of stabilization, 1/50 becomes roughly 1/3 s. But stabilization only fixes *your* movement, not the subject's.

## Long exposures and ND filters

In daylight you often can't get a slow enough shutter: even at f/22 and ISO 100, the photo is too bright. An **<Term id="nd-filter">ND filter</Term>** is dark glass that blocks a set number of stops. A 6-stop ND turns 1/60 s into 1 s; a 10-stop ND turns 1/60 into 16 s.

<Mistakes>
- Blaming the lens for "soft" photos that are actually camera shake. Check: is *everything* blurred the same way? That's shake.
- Using a tripod with stabilization on. Some systems wobble when locked down; turn it off on a tripod unless your camera detects it.
- Freezing a waterfall at 1/1000 when you wanted silk, or blurring a kid at 1/60 when you wanted them sharp. Decide first.
</Mistakes>
