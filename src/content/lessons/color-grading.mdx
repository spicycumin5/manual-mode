## Mood through color

**<Term id="split-toning">Color grading</Term>** (formerly "split toning") adds a color to the **shadows** and a different color to the **highlights**. It's how films get their recognizable looks.

- **Hue** picks the color.
- **Amount** (saturation) picks how strong. Usually 10–30 is plenty.
- **Balance** shifts the split point: favor shadows (−) or highlights (+).

## Classic grades

| Look | Shadows | Highlights |
|---|---|---|
| Teal & orange (blockbuster) | Teal ~190° | Orange ~30° |
| Warm vintage | Brown/orange ~30° | Yellow ~50° |
| Cold thriller | Blue ~220° | Neutral or light cyan |
| Pastel / dreamy | Purple ~270° | Peach ~20° |

Teal and orange works because skin tones are orange: pushing the shadows teal makes people stand out against complementary backgrounds.

## Building a consistent look

When you edit a set (a trip, a wedding, a portfolio), consistency matters more than any single photo:

1. Edit one representative photo fully.
2. Copy its settings and paste them to the others. In the Edit Lab, **Copy settings** puts the recipe on your clipboard.
3. Adjust only exposure and white balance per photo.
4. Save the recipe as a preset for next time.
