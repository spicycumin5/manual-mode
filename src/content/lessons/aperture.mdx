## The hole in the lens

Inside every lens is a ring of blades that open and close. The size of that opening is the **<Term id="aperture">aperture</Term>**, written as an **<Term id="f-number">f-number</Term>**: f/1.8, f/4, f/11…

The f-number is a fraction (focal length ÷ opening), so **a smaller number means a bigger hole**:

- **f/1.4 – f/2.8**: wide open. Lots of light, very shallow focus.
- **f/4 – f/8**: the middle. Most lenses are sharpest around f/5.6–f/8.
- **f/11 – f/22**: tiny opening. Little light, everything in focus, but detail softens from <Term id="diffraction">diffraction</Term>.

The full-stop sequence is worth memorizing: **1.4 · 2 · 2.8 · 4 · 5.6 · 8 · 11 · 16 · 22**. Each step halves the light.

## Depth of field

Aperture's side effect is **<Term id="dof">depth of field</Term>**: how much of the scene, in front of and behind your focus point, looks sharp.

Depth of field gets **shallower** (more background blur) when you:

1. **Open the aperture** (smaller f-number).
2. **Use a longer focal length**, keeping the subject the same size in the frame.
3. **Get closer** to your subject.
4. **Put more distance** between the subject and the background.

That last one is free: move your subject away from the wall and the background blurs more, no gear required.

## When to use what

| Goal | Aperture |
|---|---|
| Portrait with blurred background | f/1.4 – f/2.8 |
| Couple or small group | f/2.8 – f/4 (so both faces are sharp) |
| Street, everyday | f/5.6 – f/8 |
| Landscape, front-to-back sharp | f/8 – f/11, focused about ⅓ into the scene |
| Starbursts on lights | f/11 – f/16 |

<Callout title="Hyperfocal distance">
For landscapes, the <Term id="hyperfocal">hyperfocal distance</Term> is the focus point that makes everything from half that distance to infinity sharp. At 24mm and f/11 it's only about 1.7 m. Focus there and almost everything is sharp.
</Callout>

<Challenge id="creamy-background" />

<Mistakes>
- Shooting a group at f/1.8 and only getting one face sharp.
- Stopping down to f/22 "for maximum sharpness". Diffraction makes it softer than f/8–f/11.
- Expecting a phone-like blurry background from a wide lens far away. Get closer, use a longer lens.
</Mistakes>
