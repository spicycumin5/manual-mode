## A decision process for any shot

Every situation comes down to one question: **what matters most here?**

1. **Is something moving?** Shutter speed first.
   - Freeze it: 1/500 s or faster. Show motion: slower, with a tripod or by panning.
2. **Does depth of field matter?** Aperture first.
   - Isolate the subject: wide open. Everything sharp: f/8–f/11.
3. **Then ISO** to make the exposure work. As low as possible, as high as necessary.
4. **Check** the histogram and the highlight warning. Adjust with compensation or manual changes.
5. **Focus mode** to match: AF-C for movement, AF-S for still subjects.

When the light runs out, you have to give something up. Usually the order is: **raise ISO first** (noise is fixable), then **open the aperture** (if depth of field allows), and only then **slow the shutter** (blur isn't fixable).

<Callout title="Starting points">
- **Daylight, general**: Av, f/5.6–8, Auto ISO, min shutter 1/250
- **Portrait**: Av, f/1.8–2.8, eye-AF, AF-S or AF-C
- **Action**: M + Auto ISO, 1/1000 s, f/2.8–4, AF-C
- **Landscape**: Av or M, f/8–11, ISO 100, tripod for low light
- **Night handheld**: M, wide open, shutter at the handheld limit, Auto ISO

</Callout>
