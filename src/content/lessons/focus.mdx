## Sharp where it matters

Perfect exposure means nothing if focus is on the wrong thing. Autofocus has two decisions: **when** to focus, and **where**.

### Focus modes (when)

- **<Term id="af-s">AF-S / One-shot</Term>**: focuses once when you half-press, then locks. For still subjects: portraits, landscapes, products.
- **<Term id="af-c">AF-C / AI Servo</Term>**: keeps refocusing while you hold the button. For anything that moves: kids, pets, sports, birds.
- **AF-A / AI Focus**: the camera guesses which one you need. It's convenient but unpredictable; better to choose yourself.
- **MF**: manual focus. For stars, macro, or tricky low-light scenes.

### Focus areas (where)

- **Single point**: you choose exactly where. Precise and reliable.
- **Zone / expanded**: a small group of points, which helps track erratic subjects.
- **Wide / tracking**: the camera chooses, often following a subject around the frame.
- **Eye / face detection**: modern cameras find and track eyes. Use it for people and animals.

## Back-button focus

**<Term id="back-button">Back-button focus</Term>** moves autofocus to a button on the back of the camera, so the shutter button only takes the picture. Then:

- Hold the back button to track a moving subject (AF-C).
- Release it to lock focus, recompose, and shoot. No need to switch modes.

It feels strange for a day, then many people never go back.
