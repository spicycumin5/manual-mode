## The six light sliders

| Slider | What it does | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Exposure** | Brightens or darkens everything, in stops | Fix overall brightness first |
| **Contrast** | Pushes tones away from (or toward) the middle | Add punch, or soften harsh light |
| **Highlights** | Brightens or darkens only the bright areas | Recover a bright sky (pull down) |
| **Shadows** | Brightens or darkens only the dark areas | Open up shadow detail (push up) |
| **Whites** | Sets the white point | Make the brightest tones truly white |
| **Blacks** | Sets the black point | Make the darkest tones truly black |

A classic "starting edit": **Highlights −, Shadows +, Whites +, Blacks −**. It recovers detail in both ends, then restores contrast at the extremes. Go gently; overdone, it creates the flat, glowing "HDR" look.

<Try>
- Pull **Highlights** down to recover the lamp glow and neon.
- Push **Shadows** up to reveal the street. Watch the noise appear: shadows hide noise until you brighten them.
- Drag **Whites** and **Blacks** until the histogram just touches both edges.
</Try>

<Mistakes>
- Pushing shadows to +100 on every photo. Shadows give depth; keep some.
- Fixing exposure with Contrast or Whites. Get overall brightness right first.
- Recovering highlights that are fully clipped. If the pixel is pure white, there's nothing left to recover, which is why you protect highlights when shooting.
</Mistakes>
