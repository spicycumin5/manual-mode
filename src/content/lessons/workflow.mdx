## Why edit at all?

Every digital photo is processed by someone. Shoot JPEG and the camera makes the decisions. Shoot RAW and you do. Editing isn't cheating: it's the second half of taking the picture, the way film photographers worked in the darkroom.

## A repeatable order

Work from big to small. Most editors (Lightroom, Capture One, darktable, Photos apps) follow roughly this order:

1. **Crop & straighten**: decide the composition first.
2. **White balance**: get colors neutral (or intentionally warm/cool).
3. **Exposure**: overall brightness.
4. **Tone**: contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks.
5. **Tone curve**: fine contrast shaping and "looks".
6. **Color**: vibrance, saturation, HSL for specific colors.
7. **Color grading**: mood in shadows and highlights.
8. **Detail**: sharpening and noise reduction.
9. **Local adjustments**: brush or mask specific areas.

Each step builds on the previous one, so jumping around makes you chase your own tail.

## The histogram is your friend

The same histogram you used in camera works in editing. Watch it while you adjust:

- A histogram that doesn't reach either edge is **flat**: no true blacks or whites.
- Moving **Whites** right and **Blacks** left until the histogram just touches the edges is the fastest way to a punchy, full-range image.
- Spikes at the very edges mean you're **clipping**.
